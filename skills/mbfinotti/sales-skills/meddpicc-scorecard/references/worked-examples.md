# Worked Examples

Three worked scorecards:

- A full B2B enterprise pass.
- A thin early-stage deal shown as the negative example, annotated with why each inflated score was knocked down.
- A high-ticket B2C adaptation, whose mapping is a structural analogy rather than established MEDDPICC practice.

## 1. B2B enterprise - full eight-letter pass

Deal: warehouse-automation platform, $240K ACV, 7 known stakeholders, formal procurement + security review, negotiation stage. Right-sizing: all 8.

| Element            | Score | RAG | Claim                                                      | Evidence artefact                                                     | Next action                                                                     |
| ------------------ | ----- | --- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Metrics            | 3     | G   | Buyer expects $1.1M/yr saved from picking-error reduction  | VP Ops supplied the figure in her business-case draft (document seen) | -                                                                               |
| Economic Buyer     | 2     | A   | COO is the EB; champion has booked an intro for the 14th   | Calendar invite exists; meeting not yet held ("Access")               | Hold the meeting; confirm the case matters to him                               |
| Decision Criteria  | 3     | G   | Six criteria incl. integration cost and ops-risk reduction | Buyer's own evaluation sheet, co-edited last month                    | -                                                                               |
| Decision Process   | 2     | A   | Tech validation done; committee vote then COO sign-off     | Steps described on a recorded call; no co-edited plan                 | Send the mutual action plan; ask the buyer to edit dates                        |
| Paper Process      | 1     | R   | "Legal is fast here, two weeks max"                        | None - champion's verbal reassurance only                             | Map the path: who owns security review, MSA redlines, and their usual timelines |
| Implicate the Pain | 3     | G   | Mis-picks cost a named key account; renewal at risk        | Buyer stated the costed consequence in the QBR recording              | -                                                                               |
| Champion           | 3     | G   | Ops director has acted repeatedly                          | Booked the EB intro, shared the internal evaluation sheet             | -                                                                               |
| Competition        | 2     | A   | One named rival plus "wait until next fiscal year"         | Buyer named both on a call; no win plan against do-nothing            | Position against inertia using the buyer's own $1.1M figure                     |

Gates: Champion 3, EB 2 (identified + access) - no cap. Total: 19/24 (79%) → **Real but gapped**, one point under the forecast-ready band.

Biggest gap: **Paper Process**. The default efficiency order across the four open asks is do-nothing probe > mutual action plan > EB meeting > paper-process map, and the paper-process map still goes first: the deal is in negotiation with procurement known, the stated condition that promotes the starved ask. A week of mapping now costs a week; discovered after the verbal yes, the same work costs a stall.

Buyer-visible test: ask the champion to introduce the procurement owner this week; performed or refused, the answer scores the letter.

Deferred: the do-nothing probe and the mutual action plan are near-zero and run in parallel this week. The EB meeting is already booked for the 14th, so it costs no further champion credit and needed no promotion.

Challenged: none, every 2 and 3 named its artefact.

Re-score: after the COO meeting on the 14th.

## 2. Early-stage thin deal - negative example, annotated

Deal: analytics tool, $60K ACV, 3 stakeholders, demo stage. Right-sizing: 7 letters (Paper Process dropped - no known procurement). The rep self-scored 17/21; the anti-inflation rule rewrote it:

| Element            | Rep's claim                                                       | Rep | Final | Why it was knocked down                                                                  |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Metrics            | "They'll save at least $200K - I built the ROI model"             | 3   | 1     | Rep-built number the buyer never confirmed. No buyer-owned figure exists                 |
| Economic Buyer     | "The CFO approves everything; my contact says she's on board"     | 3   | 1     | Hearsay. EB named but never met, no intro committed - not even "Access"                  |
| Decision Criteria  | "They need dashboards, alerts, and our API - it's all in the RFP" | 3   | 1     | An RFP feature sheet treated as neutral criteria; nobody confirmed what actually decides |
| Decision Process   | "Signing by end of quarter"                                       | 2   | 1     | The close date is the rep's, not the buyer's; no described process at all                |
| Implicate the Pain | "Reporting takes them days and everyone hates it"                 | 2   | 2     | Buyer did say it on the demo call - stated, but uncosted, so not a 3                     |
| Champion           | "The data lead loves us"                                          | 3   | 1     | Enthusiasm without action: no intro, no shared criteria - a coach until tested           |
| Competition        | "No one else is in the deal"                                      | 1   | 1     | Honest, at least. Inertia unexamined; do-nothing is undefeated here                      |

Gates: Champion 1 and EB score below Access → verdict capped at **Under-qualified** regardless of total. Final: 8/21 (38%), consistent with the cap.

Biggest gap: **Champion**, a failed gate, and also the top of the efficiency line: one ask on a call already in the diary, and the answer scores Champion and Economic Buyer at once.

Buyer-visible test: ask the data lead to set up the CFO meeting; if she hesitates, she is a coach, and the deal has no champion, which is also worth knowing.

Deferred: the criteria write-down and the mutual action plan cost an hour each and would each lift a letter, but nothing they produce is trustworthy while no one inside will act.

The lesson: the rep's 17/21 and the evidence-based 8/21 describe the same deal; only one of them predicts anything.

## 3. High-ticket B2C adaptation (entire mapping is analogy)

Deal: $85K kitchen remodel, a couple, one in-home consult held, financing needed. Right-sizing: 4 letters - Metrics, Implicate the Pain, Champion, Paper Process (as financing). EB folds into Champion/spouse dynamics; formal Decision Criteria/Process are over-engineering here.

| Element (B2C reading)         | Score | RAG | Claim                                                                            | Evidence artefact                                                         | Next action                                                                         |
| ----------------------------- | ----- | --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Metrics (household stakes)    | 2     | A   | "$85K is workable if monthly stays under $900"                                   | One spouse said it at the consult; lender hasn't confirmed                | Get the pre-approval figure - the lender's number is the real metric                |
| Implicate the Pain            | 3     | G   | Hosting Thanksgiving is off for the third year; resale agent flagged the kitchen | Both spouses told the story unprompted, with the agent's estimate in hand | -                                                                                   |
| Champion (household advocate) | 2     | A   | One spouse is driving; the other worries about cost                              | Driving spouse booked the follow-up - acted once; skeptic not yet engaged | Ask the advocate to bring the skeptic to the showroom visit - the B2C EB-intro test |
| Paper Process (financing)     | 1     | R   | "Our bank will do it, we've been with them for years"                            | None - no application started                                             | Application submitted or not by Friday; that event scores the letter                |

Gates (adapted): household advocate at 2 with the money-controlling skeptic unengaged → capped at **Under-qualified**. Total: 8/12 (67%), verdict capped anyway - the percentage flatters a deal whose payer hasn't engaged.

Biggest gap: the skeptical spouse. Ranked: bring-the-skeptic ask > lender pre-approval figure > loan application.

The first is near-zero, one phone call and the B2C reading of the EB-intro test, and it tops both axes, so no promotion is needed.

The lender figure and the application are the promoted Paper Process this mapping always promotes, but they cost the household a week of its own paperwork, and nothing moves until both spouses are in.

No financing and no joint visit means "wait" wins by default; inertia is exactly as lethal in B2C.

Re-score: after the showroom visit.
