# Evidence base and benchmarks

Everything a map cites lives here with its source and caveats. Labels: **verified** (confirmed at a named source), **second-hand** (only via another publisher), **vendor-data** (a vendor's own correlational platform dataset, never independently replicated), **inference** (reasoned from the labelled figures above, not itself reported). No vendor figure below is causal; engagement co-occurs with winning, and the arrow is unproven.

## Buying-committee size

- Gartner: "the median B2B buying group involves six to 10 decision makers, each armed with four or five pieces of information" - **verified** to Gartner's _The New B2B Buying Journey_ / 2017 Digital B2B Buyer Survey (n=750). Median, complex solutions - not all B2B.
- Misquotation warning: the circulating "buyers consume 17 pieces of content" is a **verified misattribution** - Gartner's real "17" is that buyers spend only 17% of their buying time meeting with all potential suppliers combined.
- CEB: 5.4 stakeholders (2014 survey of 3,000+ B2B stakeholders, published in _The Challenger Customer_), rising to 6.8 by 2016 - **verified** as CEB's figures. The "6.8, HBR 2017" citation is the same data republished.
- Forrester _The State of Business Buying_ (2026): the typical buying decision includes 13 internal stakeholders and 9 external influencers - **verified** as Forrester's claim.
- 6sense: average buying group ~10-11 people, rising with selling price - **vendor-data** (2023 survey of 1,000+ practitioners/buyers).
- These figures differ in scope (median vs mean; "decision makers" vs "anyone involved"), not in direction: committees have grown and scale with deal value.

## Multithreading and EB engagement

- Gong (1.8M new-business deals, 2024 analysis): "multi-threading boosts win rates by an average of 130% in deals over $50K"; large strategic deals average 17 buyer contacts; "selling teams for closed-won deals are 67% larger than those for lost deals" - all **vendor-data**, verified as Gong's published claims, correlational.
- Gong (2020, n=9,056 opportunities): deals without a decision-maker (VP/CXO+) involved are 80% less likely to close; 233% for enterprise deals - **vendor-data**, relative likelihoods, not absolute win-rate points.
- Ebsta x Pavilion (2024, 4.2M opportunities): top performers are far more likely (489% in one section, 241% in a differently-scoped section of the same report) to engage the economic buyer before solution-presented; won deals average 9 engaged contacts at that stage vs 2 for lost - **vendor-data**, platform-customer selection bias.
- LinkedIn Sales Solutions (2016 internal analysis): multithreading lifts win rate 14% overall, 34% for new-business reps; deal sizes 43% lower when single-threaded - **vendor-data**, dated.
- SetSail (2021): 6+ active contacts correlated with a 62% win rate; finance contacts closed best (56%) - **vendor-data**, small vendor, incentive-program context.
- Contact-count heuristics used in the coverage check (~4-7 mid-market, ~10 for $50K-$250K, 11-17+ enterprise) are a convergence of the above plus practitioner guidance - directional, never a quota.

## The Challenger counter-current

CEB's consensus data: purchase likelihood falls from 81% with one decision maker to 55% with two, to the mid-30s at six - **verified** as CEB's published claim (_The Challenger Customer_). Gartner (2025 survey, n=632) found 74% of buying teams show "unhealthy conflict," and consensus-reaching groups are 2.5x more likely to report a high-quality deal - **verified** as Gartner's figures.

Reconciliation with the multithreading data: more stakeholders makes _buying_ harder; more seller relationships across those stakeholders correlates with winning. Multithreading manages buying-group complexity - it does not cause wins, and adding contacts for the count's sake adds consensus burden.

## Champion risk

- ~20% of B2B contacts change jobs annually - **vendor-data** (a job-change-alerting vendor citing a professional-network analysis). The same vendor class reports only ~6% of contacts ever tell a vendor they moved.
- A former champion landing at a new in-profile account is a high-value warm signal (vendor-reported lifts to close rate and deal size are **vendor-data**, promotional context).
- Countermeasures with practitioner consensus: champion redundancy, an executive sponsor whose commitment survives mid-level departures, and a shared plan document acting as institutional memory when a stakeholder inherits the evaluation - **second-hand**.

## Framework disagreements on the roles

- Miller Heiman's "Coach" is not MEDDICC's "Champion": the Coach is a guide who wants you to win and can be low-power; the MEDDICC Champion requires power and influence by definition - **second-hand** comparison, consistent across methodology publishers. Grading by evidence tier resolves the conflict: a low-power guide simply never reaches Tier-1-under-cost proof.
- Challenger rejects title/role-based mapping outright: the Mobilizer is defined by consensus-building disposition, and CEB explicitly contrasts this with Miller Heiman's role-based buying-influence mapping - **second-hand**. This skill sides with behaviour over title, which is why title inference is the floor tier.
- MEDDICC treats qualification (including champion validation) as continuous re-testing through close, versus BANT-style one-time gating - **verified** as MEDDICC's stance; the map's refresh triggers encode the continuous version.
- 30MPC's sequencing frames ("Top-Down Yo-Yo": power -> champion -> blockers; "Bottom-Up Champion Selling": champion -> power -> obstacles) are **verified** as 30MPC's named playbooks - useful when deciding which map gap to attack first. Its stated premise: "a deal without an above-the-line decision maker is a dead deal" (**verified** quote, Armand Farrokh).

## Folklore register (cite only with the label)

- "40% of supposed champions fail the champion test" - practitioner-blog figure, no traceable methodology.
- "79% of purchases require CFO approval"; "C-suite holds final veto in 22% of cases" - restated in aggregator syntheses with no traceable original.
- MAP/deal-room win-rate lifts (26%, 3x, 59/71/45%) - **vendor-data**, self-reported, engagement selects for deals already trending to win.
- CFO-involvement price thresholds (~$100K+) and "security engages whenever the product touches customer data regardless of price" - practitioner heuristics, not a sourced schedule; the second one is conservative and cheap to assume, which is why the workflow assumes it.
