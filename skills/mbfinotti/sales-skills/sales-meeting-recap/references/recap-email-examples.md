# Recap email examples

All examples follow the 3-step recap structure (30MPC / Armand Farrokh): next steps first, problems not features, one phone screen. Bodies are plain text as they would be sent. Companies and people are fictional.

## Strong example - B2B mid-market, post-demo

Context: Nora (AE at Brightpath, a data-quality platform) just ran a demo for Harbor Logistics. Attendees: Dana Whitfield (VP Ops, champion) and Marcus Lee (IT lead). Deal is roughly $70K ACV with a buying committee; the technical review is booked.

```
Subject: Next steps - Harbor + Brightpath

Hi Dana,

Great walking through the platform with you and Marcus today. Next steps first:

- Nora: send the completed security questionnaire and SOC 2 report - Thursday, March 12
- Dana: confirm Marcus has sandbox credentials, and loop in Priya from procurement - Monday, March 16
- All: technical review with Marcus's team - Tuesday, March 17, 10:00 (invite sent)

Why this matters: you said the ops team "loses about 12 hours a week re-keying shipment data between the TMS and billing", and that those errors triggered the Q2 audit finding. The technical review is where Marcus pressure-tests whether we remove that re-keying entirely.

I've attached the integration one-pager Marcus asked for.

Anything I missed?

Nora
```

Why it passes the gate, in the same order the skill ranks these elements:

- Action items sit at the top. Every one carries Owner + Deliverable + Date, and the buyer's item is a visible line of its own.
- The next meeting has a real date, a time, and a sent calendar invite.
- The pain is quoted verbatim - the buyer's phrasing, the buyer's metric, tied to their compelling event (the audit finding).
- The recap widens the thread: Dana is asked to bring in procurement, and Marcus is CC-worthy - not single-threaded.
- The closing question invites ratification: a one-word reply turns this into an agreed record.
- Subject is 30 characters and next-step-anchored.
- The attachment is named but never listed as an action item.
- Roughly 120 words - one phone screen, plain text throughout, rendering identically in any email client.

## Negative example - what not to do

The same call, done wrong:

```
Subject: Following up on our great conversation today and next steps for the Brightpath evaluation!

Hi Dana,

Thank you so much for taking the time to meet with me today! It was wonderful to connect and learn more about Harbor.

I wanted to recap some of the powerful capabilities we covered: our AI-powered matching engine, 200+ pre-built connectors, real-time validation rules, customizable role-based dashboards, and our award-winning support team. We really believe Brightpath can transform the way your team works.

I'll circle back next week to see where things stand. In the meantime, don't hesitate to reach out if you need anything at all - always happy to jump on a call!

I'm also attaching our full product deck, pricing overview, implementation guide, and customer story library for your review.

Looking forward to working together!
```

Defect by defect:

- Subject runs past 90 characters and says "next steps" while the email contains none.
- Zero action items. "I'll circle back next week" is a seller-only step with no date, no deliverable, and nothing for the buyer to do.
- No calendar invite and no next meeting - momentum has nowhere to live.
- Feature dump replaces the buyer's stated pain.
- The 12-hours-a-week quote and the audit finding, the actual reasons Dana took the call, never appear.
- Single-threaded: nothing widens the deal to Marcus or procurement.
- Four attachments dumped at once, none of them the one Marcus asked for.
- No closing question, so there is nothing for the buyer to ratify.
- Templated enthusiasm ("wonderful to connect", "transform the way your team works") reads as automation and buries the two questions a recap must answer: what happens next, and why the buyer agreed to it.

## Short-motion example - transactional / B2C

Context: high-ticket B2C - a home solar consultation. Single decision-making household, short cycle. Same rules, resized: 3-5 sentences, one next step with a real date, no mutual close plan.

```
Subject: Your quote + next step

Hi Sam,

Thanks for the walkthrough today. You mentioned the $310 summer electric bills are the main driver - the attached proposal is sized against that usage.

One next step: a 20-minute review call Thursday at 5:30pm to go through the numbers together - invite sent. If Thursday doesn't work, reply with a better slot.

Anything I missed?

Alex
```

Notes:

- The buyer's stated driver appears in their own words.
- The single next step has a date and an invite.
- The fallback ask is a forced choice, not "whenever works".
- A close plan here would be pure friction: the booked call is the whole plan.
