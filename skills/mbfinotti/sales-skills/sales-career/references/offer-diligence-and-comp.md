## B2C compensation shapes

Sub-ranking across the four verticals, for a user who has already chosen the B2C world. Default ordering:

- efficiency (income bought per year of unpaid runway): auto > solar > insurance > real estate
- income within two years: auto (median ~$68,400) > insurance == solar > real estate ($8,000 newest-agent median). The tie is an argued opt-out, not a dodge: both are reported only as overlapping ranges from thin trade sources, so ordering them against each other would be false precision.
- effort (licensing, unpaid runway, washout, hours): real estate > insurance == auto > solar. Insurance and auto tie on survival odds - 89% gone within three years and 40-70% annually compound to the same place - and differ in hours, not odds. Solar sits lowest on documented cost (no licence, recoverable draw), not on better odds; no reliable turnover figure exists for it.
- compliance cost: real estate and insurance require a jurisdiction-bound licence, continuing education, and brokerage sponsorship in real estate - relocating restarts it. Solar and auto require none.

What this order starves: real estate, whose book compounds for decades (16+ years median $88,500) behind the worst first two years on the list. Promote it when the user has a local network, 12+ months of runway, and answered "compounding asset" in the interview. Delete a licensed vertical outright, naming the constraint, when the user will not or cannot licence in their jurisdiction - never rank it last.

- **Real estate**(NAR 2026 Member Profile): member median gross income $59,200 (2025). Newest agents (2 years or less): median $8,000 on a median 2 transaction sides - the first year is effectively unpaid apprenticeship. 16+ years: median $88,500.
- **Insurance**: 10-20% of first-year P&C premium plus 2-15% renewals; some agencies pay salary-plus-commission for 12-18 months before going commission-heavy. Roughly 89% of new agents gone within three years(trade sources - treat as directional).
- **Solar** /,): 5-20% commission or flat per-sale/per-kW, usually on a recoverable draw; door-to-door income commonly $50-120K; final-pay disputes over draw balances are common.
- **Auto**: draw plus 20-25% of front-end gross, back-end F&I, manufacturer spiffs; median ~$68,400; 40-70% annual turnover, 50-60+ hour weeks, monthly quota resets.

B2C data is thinner and less rigorous than B2B - tell the user so whenever advising from it.

## Offer evaluation workflow

1. Build a percentile band (25th/50th/75th) for the exact role, level, and location from RepVue, Glassdoor, and Levels.fyi; note each source and pull date. Location always matters - specify it.
2. Re-value the OTE at the team's real attainment, not 100%: expected variable = variable x team attainment rate. Against the ~43% market baseline(crowdsourced), a $200K OTE on a 30%-attainment team is not a $200K offer.
3. Compare total compensation, not base: base + variable-at-real-attainment + equity + benefits, in a side-by-side table when offers compete.
4. Ask the diligence questions below before deciding; score any refusal as data.
5. B2C: run the draw-terms checklist; no written terms, no acceptance.

## Diligence questions

Ask them top-down. Ordering by decision value: team attainment (Q1) > quota capacity against company goal (Q2) > ramp quota (Q3) == last quota raise (Q4). Q3 and Q4 tie because each moves the same number - your first-year expected variable - and neither can override what Q1 already answered. For B2C, the lead-flow question (Q5) ranks alongside Q1: a commission seat with no lead flow has no attainment worth asking about.

1. "What percentage of people in this exact role, on this exact team, hit quota last quarter?" Attainment varies team by team inside one company - check the team, not the logo.
2. "How many reps carry what quota, against what company goal?" Quota capacity far above the company goal means over-hiring ahead of demand.
3. "What is quota during ramp, and how long is ramp?"
4. "When were quotas last raised, and by how much?"
5. B2C: "What is the lead flow - who generates it, and what share of my pipeline is it expected to be?"

Interpretation(one VC diligence framework): aggregate attainment 60-70% is "bad numbers"; 85-100% is "really good". A manager who will not state last quarter's team attainment is a red flag regardless of the OTE.

## Offer red flags

- Big headline OTE with no attainment data offered - the documented pattern is to "throw out big OTE numbers" while omitting terrible attainment.
- Resume-stage comp questions deflected, then a lowball anchored on your current salary.
- B2B: quota assigned before territory or ramp terms are defined; "everyone hits quota here".
- B2C: recoverable draw with unwritten recovery terms; negative balance silently deducted from final pay; "unlimited earning potential" as the only comp statement.
- Any verbal promise (territory, accounts, promotion timeline) missing from the written offer.

## B2C draw-terms checklist - all in writing before signing

1. Draw amount, and recoverable vs non-recoverable.
2. Recovery schedule: which commissions repay the draw, at what rate.
3. Negative-balance treatment at separation - the main source of final-pay disputes.
4. Clawback conditions on cancelled or refunded sales, and their window.
5. Lead flow commitment, if any, in measurable terms.
6. Personal runway check: 3-6 months of savings before straight commission, independent of what the employer promises.
