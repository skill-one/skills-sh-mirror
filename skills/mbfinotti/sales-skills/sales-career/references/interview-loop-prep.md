# Interview loop prep (candidate side)

Everything here applies to B2B and B2C alike unless a row says otherwise: the improvisation exercises, the value of quantified proof, and the resilience/coachability signal are identical in both worlds (convergence).

## The loop, stage by stage

| Stage              | Typical length | What is tested                                      | How to prepare                                                         |
| ------------------ | -------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Recruiter screen   | 15-30 min      | Basic fit, quota history, comp expectations         | One-liner versions of your top stories; a deflection-ready comp answer |
| Behavioral (STAR)  | 45-60 min      | Evidence of the competencies below                  | The story bank, rehearsed at all three lengths                         |
| Skills / role-play | 30-45 min      | Discovery, objection handling, composure, the close | Mock-call plan; rehearse aloud, not silently                           |
| Panel / final      | 60-90 min      | Strategic thinking, fit, questions you ask          | Company research; the 30-60-90 plan if requested                       |
| References         | -              | Facts, yes/no                                       | Brief each reference on the exact numbers you claimed                  |

Stage structure is from published sales-hiring guides; treat lengths as typical, not fixed.

## Objection handling

_How would you deal with a dream client who is being difficult?_ (Close.com)
_What's a problem you faced where you had to think outside the box to solve it?_ (Salesforce)
_Give me an example of a time when you were forced to comply with a policy you didn't agree with. How did you handle it?_ (Gong - leadership, but applies to objection handling)

## Strengtheners for your interview

- Named target accounts (B2B) or a named farming area/lead source plan (B2C).
- Specific numbers with their benchmark source.
- A stated cadence for manager check-ins.

## Weakener: generic phase labels with no numbers - it reads as a template found online.

## Brag book

A tabbed portfolio, presented when performance comes up:

- Resume.
- Sales numbers.
- Rankings.
- Awards.
- Reviews.
- Reference letters.

Near-mandatory in medical/pharma sales, differentiating everywhere else. Every number in it must have passed the evidence audit; a brag book is a verification target, not a decoration.

## Questions to ask the interviewer

Ask at least two, taking them from the top - they test transparency and generate diligence data at the same time. They are in ask-first order; the axes behind that order live in offer-diligence-and-comp.md § Diligence questions, and for B2C the draw question ranks with the first.

1. "What percentage of people in this role, on this team, hit quota last quarter?"
2. "How many reps carry what quota, against what company goal?" (Detects over-hired quota capacity.)
3. "What did the last person promoted out of this role do, and how long did it take?"
4. "What does ramp look like, and how is quota treated during it?"
5. B2C: "Walk me through the draw - recovery schedule, and what happens to a negative balance if I leave."

## Avoid:

- Questions answerable by the website.
- Pure comp questions before an offer.
- Yes/no questions.

## Weak answers that lose the room

- Feature-dumping in any role-play; pitching before a single question.
- "I like talking to people" as the motivation for sales disqualifier).
- Generic stories with no numbers, or numbers that do not cross-multiply.
- Claiming 100%+ attainment in every period - itself a red flag.
- No questions for the panel, or only comp questions.
- Passivity: never asking for the close, in the role-play or for the job itself.
