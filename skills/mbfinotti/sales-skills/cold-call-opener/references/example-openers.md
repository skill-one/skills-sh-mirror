# Worked example openers

Illustrations of the anatomy, not measured winners: no call data ranks these particular scripts, so rewrite them for the actual list and offer rather than reading them verbatim. Every one passed the self-check gate in SKILL.md.

## Example 1 - B2B, permission-based, verified signal

Persona: VP Sales at a 200-person software company. Verified signal: six open SDR job postings. Product one-liner: "we keep outbound contact data current so new reps don't dial dead numbers". Desired outcome: booked meeting.

> "Hi, this is Dana Whitfield with Meridian Data. I'll be upfront - this is a cold call, but I saw you've got six SDR openings up, so I think it's a relevant one. Can I take twenty seconds to tell you why I called, and you can tell me if it's off base?"

Why it works, component by component:

- **Identification** (3s): full name, company, said plainly.
- **Owns the interruption**: "this is a cold call" - no apology, no fake warmth. This is the permission-based pattern's core move.
- **Reason-for-call carries a verified signal**: the job postings are public, checkable, and connected to the product - not trivia personalization.
- **Ask** (one): twenty seconds of permission, with an explicit off-ramp ("tell me if it's off base") that makes "no" cheap and therefore "yes" easy.
- What is deliberately absent: the pain sentence. In this variant the pain lands _after_ permission is granted ("Teams ramping that many reps usually find a third of their list data is stale by month two - is that showing up for you?"). Isolating whether pain-before-permission or pain-after-permission works better for this persona is exactly the kind of one-variable test the skill's test plan runs.
- Timer: ~13 seconds.

## Example 2 - B2B, direct reason-for-call, guessed pain (hedged)

Persona: Head of Operations at a mid-size logistics firm. No verified signal - the pain is an educated guess from pattern across similar accounts. Desired outcome: qualification.

> "Hi, this is Marco Reyes at Fieldstone Software. The reason for my call is - most ops leads I talk to at carriers your size tell me their dispatchers still juggle three screens to confirm one delivery. I'm guessing some version of that is on your plate too, but you'd know better than me. Way off, or close?"

Why it works:

- **Reason-for-call pattern**: the John Barrows test passes - the sentence "the reason for my call is..." completes concretely.
- **Hedged pain line**: the guess is framed as peer-reported ("most ops leads I talk to..."), then explicitly handed over ("you'd know better than me"). Disagreeing costs the prospect nothing, so the conversation survives a "no". An unhedged version ("I know your dispatchers are drowning") invites the top-five brush-off "I don't have the problem you're describing" (Cognism 2025).
- **Concrete pain vocabulary**: "juggle three screens to confirm one delivery" is something an ops lead would say out loud. "Operational inefficiencies in your delivery-confirmation workflow" is not.
- **Ask** (one): "Way off, or close?" - a binary the prospect can answer in one breath, which is the qualification outcome this rep wanted.
- Timer: ~19 seconds.

## Example 3 - B2C, direct reason-for-call, consented list

Persona: homeowner who submitted a quote-request form for home insurance nine days ago. Jurisdiction: US; number scrubbed against the national and internal do-not-call lists; the form is the consent basis. Desired outcome: complete the quote on this call.

> "Hi, is this Karen? This is Sam Okafor from Brightway Insurance - you asked for a home insurance quote on our site last Tuesday and I'm the one who finally gets to call you back. Most folks tell us they just want to know if they're overpaying without sitting through a sales pitch. Have you got three or four minutes to run the numbers now?"

Why it works, and where B2C diverges from B2B:

- **The consent event is the reason-for-call**: "you asked for a quote last Tuesday" replaces any manufactured relevance. Same anatomy slot, different content - this is the one structural difference a consented B2C list creates.
- **Household vocabulary**: "overpaying", "sales pitch", "run the numbers" - money and hassle in plain words, zero industry language. A B2B pain line here would be a defect.
- **The ask continues the same call** ("run the numbers now") instead of booking a future meeting - the typical B2C outcome shape.
- **Time ask is honest**: three or four minutes is what a quote takes. Asking for less to get in the door fails the self-check gate.
- Identical to B2B: the four components, the length budget, one ask, the pause after the ask.
- Timer: ~20 seconds.

## Negative example - annotated, do not imitate

Same persona as Example 2. Every line fails a different way:

> "Hi Rachel! How have you been? I know you're super busy so I'm so sorry to bother you - I'll be quick, I promise. I'm calling from Fieldstone, we're the leading AI-powered logistics-orchestration platform with real-time multi-modal visibility and predictive ETA. Did I catch you at a bad time? I'd love to get thirty minutes on your calendar - also, are you the right person for this, and who handles your dispatch tooling?"

Line by line:

- **"How have you been?" to a stranger**: fabricated familiarity. Even where Gong's 2018 data favored the line, it favored it as a pattern interrupt: faking a prior relationship is a different act. The 2024 study scores small talk as telemarketer camouflage, and Josh Braun's objection (manufactured familiarity) applies in full.
- **"So sorry to bother you"**: apologizing for calling concedes the call is a bother. The permission-based pattern owns the interruption without apology.
- **No identification**: first name never given, company name mumbled into a feature dump.
- **Feature dump**: "AI-powered logistics-orchestration platform with real-time multi-modal visibility" is the vendor's internal vocabulary. No prospect has ever said those words out loud. There is no pain sentence at all.
- **"Did I catch you at a bad time?"**: invites the scripted exit. Gong 2018: 0.9% success, 40% below baseline; Gong 2024: 2.15%, roughly baseline. The datasets disagree on how bad it is - both agree better options exist.
- **"Thirty minutes on your calendar"**: asking for time the conversation has not earned, before relevance is established.
- **Stacked asks**: calendar slot + "are you the right person" + "who handles dispatch tooling" - three asks in one breath. The prospect answers none of them.
- **Length**: ~24 seconds of uninterrupted talk with no pause planned - and every one of those seconds spent on the rep, not the prospect.
