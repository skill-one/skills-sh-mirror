# Opener patterns and the evidence behind them

Two Gong datasets carry nearly all the published weight on opener wording, and they contradict each other. Read the contradictions section before quoting any number to a user.

## The patterns

Evidence inventory, not a ranking. Rows follow the efficiency order in SKILL.md § Choosing the pattern so the two never contradict each other. The value and effort axes, and which of these the skill actually offers, are decided there.

Also documented, but not opener-specific:

- **Sandler's up-front contract** (David Sandler, 1967): agree purpose, both agendas, time, and permitted outcomes including "no". A meeting-framing device. Compressing it into a cold call's first 30 seconds is an adaptation, not what Sandler specified, and it has not been measured on cold calls.
- **Jason Bay's intro-hook-close structure** (Future of Prospecting): a permission-based intro that front-loads relevance, a "priority drop" naming something the prospect is likely dealing with, then an actionable question. Bay argues against "Have you heard of [company]?" because it centers the rep, not the prospect. His REPLY messaging model defines personalization as something specific to the prospect _and connected to how the rep can help_ - unconnected personalization is trivia.
- **Jeb Blount's "5-Step Telephone Framework"** (from _Fanatical Prospecting_): the name is verified on the author's own site; the five steps are not publicly documented - never invent them.

## Gong Labs, 2018 (Chris Orlob)

90,380 outbound connected cold calls, NLP-analyzed from a 1M+ recording corpus. Success = a follow-up meeting actually held. Baseline: 1.48-1.5%.

- "How have you been?" - 10.01%, a 6.6x lift
- "How are you?" - 5.2%, a 3.4x lift
- "The reason for my call is..." - 2.1x lift; Gong describes it as pairable with any opener
- "Did I catch you at a bad time?" - 0.9%, i.e. 40% less likely to book than baseline

## Gong Labs x 30MPC, 2024 (Armand Farrokh)

Corpus described as 300M+ cold calls. Published alongside the 30MPC book release.

- Context-first / peer-proof opener - 11.24%
- Permission-based opener - 11.18%
- "How's your day going?" - 7.6%, called a weak opener despite the number
- "Did I catch you at a bad time?" - 2.15%, described as slightly above baseline

## The contradictions - report these, never smooth them

1. **The bad-time opener flips sign.** 0.9% and below-baseline in 2018; 2.15% and above-baseline in 2024. Either the baseline roughly doubled or the success definition changed - Gong publishes neither the 2024 definition nor per-opener sample sizes. Unverified either way.
2. **Small talk is scored twice, oppositely.** 2018 treats "How are you?" at 5.2% as a genuine win; 2024 treats the near-identical "How's your day going?" at 7.6% as telemarketer camouflage. The number went up while the verdict went down. Josh Braun rejects the 2018 winner outright as manufactured familiarity, data notwithstanding.
3. **"300M+" is the corpus, not the tested sample.** Per-opener n and confidence intervals are undisclosed; the 11.24% vs 11.18% gap is 0.06 points and should not be treated as separable (an inference - Gong does not say this).
4. **A propagation error to avoid repeating:** several secondary sites attribute Gong's 40% penalty to permission-based openers. It belongs to "Did I catch you at a bad time?" - a bad-timing check, not a permission ask. Never repeat the misattribution.
5. **Never merge Gong with Cognism.** Gong's success metric is meetings from connected calls; Cognism's is conversation-to-meeting. Different measures, different years, different populations - no shared baseline exists.

## Cognism / WHAM, State of Cold Calling (funnel data, no opener-wording tests)

| Metric                                 | 2024  | 2025 | 2026                  |
| -------------------------------------- | ----- | ---- | --------------------- |
| Success rate (conversation -> meeting) | 4.82% | 2.3% | 2.7%                  |
| Calls to reach a prospect              | -     | 2.9  | 1.55                  |
| Average call duration                  | ~83s  | ~93s | 82s                   |
| Cognism internal team                  | -     | 6.7% | 11.3% (16% in Europe) |

The 2025 edition: 41,936 connected calls producing 27,513 conversations, a 65.6% connect-to-conversation rate computed from those primary figures. Three attempts is optimal (93% of conversations happen by call 3).

The 2026 edition adds Thursday as the strongest day and 10:00-11:00 as the best window. Widely-circulated claims that 2.3% is a _dial_-to-meeting rate are a definitional error: it is conversation-to-meeting.

## Personalization: what is measured vs what is doctrine

- Measured (email, not calls): Gong found company-specific personalization triples prospecting-email reply rates for directors and above, with little correlation for lower-level buyers. Transferring this to live calls is an inference. The seniority conditionality is the useful part: tailoring pays where the buyer owns strategic priorities. Uniform personalization across the org chart misallocates effort.
- Doctrine (unmeasured): all persona-tailoring guidance, including which purpose fits which seniority. Present it as practice, not finding.
- Backfire risk: peer-reviewed work on the personalization backfire effect finds that under high privacy concern, heavy PII-based personalization crosses a perceived-intrusiveness threshold (consumer-marketing context, not cold calls). Practical rule: personalize on business-visible signals, never on personal details that make a stranger's knowledge feel like surveillance.
