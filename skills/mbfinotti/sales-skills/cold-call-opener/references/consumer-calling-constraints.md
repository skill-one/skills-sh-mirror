# Consumer (B2C) cold-calling constraints, by jurisdiction

The B2B/B2C difference in cold calling is not technique - it is consent architecture. A consumer number is a residential or mobile line by default, which triggers the strictest tier of every regime, and there is no business-purpose framing to fall back on. Not legal advice - have counsel confirm before any consumer campaign.

Before dialing any B2C list, regardless of jurisdiction:

1. Name the jurisdiction of the numbers being called (from the interview).
2. Scrub against that jurisdiction's registers and the internal do-not-call list.
3. Record the consent basis per number where the regime requires one.
4. Honor any "stop calling me" immediately, on that call, not at the next list refresh.

## United States

- The FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule has an explicit B2B exemption (§310.6(b)(7), with a carve-back for nondurable office/cleaning supplies). The TCPA has **no** equivalent statutory B2B exemption, and its consent requirements for calls to cell phones apply whether the call is B2B or consumer.
- Mixed-use mobile numbers are the exposure: the Ninth Circuit's _Chennette_ decision gave such plaintiffs standing on do-not-call claims.
- Florida, Oklahoma, Washington and Maryland run mini-TCPA statutes with no B2B exemption.
- Every business making telemarketing calls - B2B included - must keep an internal do-not-call list.
- The Supreme Court's June 2025 _McLaughlin Chiropractic v. McKesson_ ruling removed the requirement that district courts defer to FCC interpretations of the TCPA, so district-level outcomes vary more than before.

## European Union

- ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC, Article 13: prior consent is required for automated calling machines. Live voice calls are left to national legislation, which chooses between consent (opt-in) and objection (opt-out) regimes. Live B2C calling rules therefore differ per member state and must be checked for the specific country.
- GDPR applies to the underlying personal data regardless. A lawful basis is required (commonly legitimate interests with a documented balancing test), and Article 21 gives an unconditional right to object to direct marketing.

## United Kingdom

- PECR 2003: live marketing calls generally do not require consent, but the number must not be on the TPS (individuals, sole traders, some partnerships) or CTPS (companies, government bodies) registers, and the subscriber must not have previously objected. B2B calling therefore requires screening **both** registers plus the internal do-not-call list.
- Registration takes 28 days to take effect. Claims-management and pension calls need consent. ICO guidance is under review following the Data (Use and Access) Act.

## France

- The regime inverted in August 2026: under loi n° 2025-594 (30 June 2025) completed by décret n° 2026-662 (23 July 2026), from **11 August 2026** no consumer may be called without prior consent, and the Bloctel do-not-call list is abolished.
- Consent must be:
  - Free.
  - Specific.
  - Informed.
  - Unambiguous.
  - Revocable.
  - Valid at most one year.

  The caller bears the burden of proof. The administrative fine reaches 375,000 EUR for a legal person, plus nullity of any contract concluded on the call.

- Prohibited outright: energy renovation sales, home adaptation sales (disability/ageing), and CPF training-account sales.
- Scope is the consumer only: French professional-to-professional calling stays under GDPR plus sector rules, not this opt-in regime.
