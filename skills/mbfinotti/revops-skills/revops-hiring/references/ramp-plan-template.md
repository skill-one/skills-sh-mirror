# Ramp Plan Template

The core principle: "You weren't hired to fix [the CRM]. You were hired to orchestrate revenue." The skeleton below synthesizes this insight with generic onboarding practices and RevOps practitioner guidance.

## Sequencing rules

1. **Day 1 light, Week 2 deep.** Day 1 is setup and relationships; substantive ownership starts Week 2. Overloading Day 1 is a named trap.
2. **Assign a buddy who is not the manager.**
3. **Branch on seniority** - time to productivity runs 4-6 months junior, 2-4 mid, 1-3 senior, 3-6 executive, so the same calendar horizons carry very different expectations.
4. **Branch on archetype** - a systems hire, an analyst, and a strategist should not get the same first deliverable.
5. **End with watch areas** - the early warning signals, specific to this hire's profile, that they are struggling. A distinct section from the goals.
6. Gate connector-dependent steps on capability, never on a named product: if an HR system is reachable, auto-populate the access list; if a knowledge base is, link the existing docs; otherwise ask.

## Skeleton

- **Pre-start**: welcome email with logistics, account setup, equipment, calendar and recurring-meeting invitations, named buddy.
- **Day 1**, hour by hour (time / activity / with whom): orientation, tool setup, team intros, company overview, an explicit slot walking through role expectations and this 30-60-90 plan itself, unstructured exploration time.
- **Week 1** checklist:
  - Compliance training.
  - Role-specific reading.
  - A 1:1 with every GTM stakeholder.
  - Shadowing key meetings.
  - One small first task.
  - End-of-week manager check-in.
  - RevOps-specific access list, granted in week one, not on request: CRM admin permissions, a CRM sandbox, data warehouse and BI access, the marketing automation platform, and a seat in the meetings that matter (weekly pipeline, the forecast call, the monthly funnel review, QBRs).
  - Consequence of skipping the access list: a RevOps hire who can't see your real pipeline data, get a direct line to the VP of Sales, and make changes to the CRM without a 3-week approval process is a RevOps hire who will fail slowly.
- **30 / 60 / 90-day goals**: three separate numbered lists, written from the scorecard's ranked outcomes - never generic.
- **Key contacts** table (manager, buddy, IT, HR, executive sponsor - each with "for what") and a **tools access** table (tool / access level / requested).
- **Watch areas**: last section, per the rules above.

## Honest milestones

Practitioner-sourced timeline:

- 60-90 days: early wins, cleaner data and better reporting.
- 6 months: structural improvements, process documentation, CRM architecture changes.
- 12+ months: measurable outcomes at the revenue level.

The management implication is explicit: do not fire a good hire at month six for lack of revenue movement, and "Anyone promising revenue impact in 30 days is selling you something."

## Archetype branches

- **Systems hire**: first deliverable is a stack and data-quality audit with a prioritized fix list - not a rebuild. Watch area: heads-down configuration with no stakeholder interviews.
- **Analyst**: first deliverable is a baseline report leadership actually trusts (pipeline or funnel), with definitions written down. Watch area: producing dashboards nobody asked for instead of answering the questions leadership has.
- **Strategist**: first deliverable is a stakeholder-interview synthesis and a process map of one revenue motion, with a proposed operating cadence. Watch area: strategy documents with no shipped change by day 60 - the wrong-altitude failure where "sprints don't ship".

## Greenfield vs existing team

- **Greenfield (no predecessor)**: ramps _diagnostically_ - stakeholder interviews, systems audit, data-quality baselines - and tolerance for ambiguity is a hiring criterion in its own right.
- **Existing team**: week one shifts toward absorbing documented processes and standards instead.

## Defend the calendar

Protect the first 90 days from the ticket queue: "if their first 90 days are spent closing tickets, they will never build the strategic muscle you hired them for." Agree with the sponsor, before day one, what share of time goes to the queue and who absorbs the rest.

Do not cite general tenure statistics (e.g. BLS median tenure) as if they apply to RevOps; they do not.
