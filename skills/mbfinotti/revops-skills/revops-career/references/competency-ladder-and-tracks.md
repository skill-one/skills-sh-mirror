## The five-rung consensus ladder

Synthesized across RevOps Co-op, Fullcast, The GTM Advisor Group and RevenueTools. Use scope, decision rights, altitude and headcount to name a level - never the title alone.

| Level                              | Years  | Scope                                                                                                 | Decision rights                     | Stakeholder altitude                                 | Headcount |
| ---------------------------------- | ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | --------- |
| Analyst / Coordinator / Specialist | 0-3    | Workflows, reports, CRM hygiene                                                                       | Executes defined tasks              | Team leads, ops peers                                | 0         |
| Manager                            | 3-7    | Owns a platform or function; territory and quota planning, forecasting workflows, comp administration | Owns process, scopes projects       | Department heads                                     | 0-3       |
| Senior Manager / Director          | 5-10   | Sets the RevOps roadmap and GTM strategy, owns the stack                                              | Prioritizes across GTM, owns budget | VP Sales / Marketing / CS; boardroom exposure begins | 3-15      |
| VP / Head of RevOps                | 8+     | Owns the revenue engine operationally                                                                 | Company-wide metric ownership       | CRO / CEO / COO; boardroom                           | 15-50+    |
| CRO-adjacent                       | Varies | Full revenue architecture                                                                             | Executive                           | Board                                                | Org       |

Senior individual contributors often evolve into chief-of-staff-shaped roles supporting a CRO or CFO, focused on strategic revenue planning rather than tactical execution (Jose Aleman, VP GTM Excellence at Everstage, RevOps Co-op 2026 salary report webinar).

## The IC-versus-manager fork

This fork, not the ladder, is the real career decision.

- Managers and directors out-earn even senior ICs, and the manager transition is the single largest compensation step in the field (RevOps Co-op 2025 Compensation and Impact Report, 1,200+ respondents, with BoostUp).
- A genuine senior IC track (Staff, Principal) exists mainly at enterprise scale, roughly 500+ employees. Below that, "senior IC" usually means the same job with a bigger title.
- The clearest published example of both tracks side by side is GitLab's public handbook, which documents an Individual Contributor Track and a Management Track with Staff, Principal, Distinguished and Fellow rungs, and treats Sales Operations, Sales Strategy and Customer Success Operations as separate job families.
- Management test, from RevenueTools: "If you cannot hire, develop, and retain talent, you are a senior IC with a management title."
- Warn the candidate about the hybrid trap: many RevOps managers carry both management and IC work, which is the documented burnout path (RevOps Co-op 2026 salary report webinar). Ask whether a target role is a real management seat or an IC seat with reports attached.

## Title inflation is measured, not folklore

- RevenueTools reports VP of Revenue Operations titles grew 300% in 18 months.
- Danielle Marquis, a 15-year RevOps leader speaking on the RevOps FM podcast: "there's a huge difference between being a VP RevOps leading a team of 10 at a 300 person company and one leading a 50-person org for one business unit at a 10,000 person company. They are not the same job."
- Mahak Vedi (CEO, Revology Consulting) frames the wider problem as a RevOps identity crisis: "RevOps became something of a catchall." Her self-reported count, not independently verified, is roughly 7,000 RevOps roles and 1,200-1,400 go-to-market engineering roles posted globally as of around December 2025.
- Practical consequence: do not job-search by title. Filter by underlying function - analysis, process design, systems ownership, cross-functional decision rights.

## The archetype matrix

Jared Barol's RevOps Archetype Matrix, co-created with Steven Daniels (RevOps Co-op podcast episode 66). Two axes: focus (technical vs. business) crossed with orientation (tactical vs. strategic).

| Archetype         | Position              | What they do                                                  | Growth wing                                      |
| ----------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Systems Architect | Technical + tactical  | Translates business requirements into scalable infrastructure | Product management, CTO-style leadership         |
| Insight Ops       | Technical + strategic | Data-led, drives clean process execution                      | Ops Director, Platform Architect                 |
| GTM Operator      | Business + tactical   | Keeps sellers and marketers productive day to day             | Functional leadership in Sales, CS or Enablement |
| Growth Catalyst   | Business + strategic  | Reads market dynamics, ICP strategy, org design               | CRO, COO, President                              |

Barol's own framing matters as much as the grid: "The idea isn't to put people in boxes - it's to show how complementary archetypes can create balance."

On growth: "Just because you're tactical doesn't mean you need to become strategic. The fastest growth comes from leaning into your superpower while hiring for your gaps."

His companion OPS lens describes what a company asks of RevOps by maturity:

- **Operations** - systems, data, analytics.
- **Performance** - enablement, measurement, people and process management.
- **Strategy** - where to play, how to win.

Early-stage companies reward adaptability. Late-stage companies reward scalability and precision. Use it to check whether a target company will actually let the candidate work at their preferred altitude.

## The five career tracks

RevOps Co-op's track taxonomy. Titles vary by company; tracks do not.

The ceiling is common to all five. Every track converges on the same requirement to reach VP or C-level: "RevOps leaders must be able to network, manage up, and influence without authority."

That seat pays $216,571 at VP/SVP level (RevOps Co-op 2025 Compensation and Impact Report, Nov-2024), or $187,000-$300,000+ for a leader at a top firm (RevOps Co-op 2026 salary report). Do not attach a per-track compensation band to any of the five - that is false precision.

The largest single compensation step is the management transition, not seniority inside an IC seat. Rank the tracks by how far each sits from that step, and by what each keeps open for a candidate who never takes it.

- efficiency (management seat reached per year of re-skilling): project manager > analyst > systems manager > enablement > deal desk
- value (ceiling reached, and the exits kept open on the way): systems manager > analyst > project manager > enablement > deal desk
- effort (re-skilling to reach a management seat): deal desk > systems manager > enablement > analyst > project manager
- time to seniority (calendar years to the manager rung): deal desk > systems manager == enablement > analyst == project manager

| Track                           | Fits someone who                                                       | Common feeder roles                                                        | Years to the manager rung                                                                  | What gates the common ceiling                                                                                                                                                        | Market depth                                                                                                                             | Exit paths                                                            |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Project Manager                 | Gathers requirements and drives change management                      | Ex-analysts, admins with project experience                                | 3-7, entered laterally with no new stack                                                   | Nothing technical - influence without authority is the whole gate, and it is the bar this track already trains against                                                               | Not separately sized; the track rides whatever RevOps hiring exists in the candidate's own market                                        | Management and director track                                         |
| Analyst                         | Is data-obsessed and asks why a lot                                    | Financial analyst, accountant, SDR, digital marketing                      | 3-7, plus months of SQL and data rigour when the base is missing                           | Finance fluency: "show me a RevOps person who can manage data and speak the language of finance, and I'll show you somebody who is in the boardroom in three weeks" (Stephen Diorio) | Widened by adjacent go-to-market-engineering demand, which grew sharply through 2025-26 on postings analyses rather than audited surveys | RevOps leadership, or a pure data track                               |
| Systems Manager / Administrator | Is efficiency-minded and UI-curious                                    | GTM end users who became "the systems person"                              | 3-7 to manage, on top of years of platform depth first, and only once the fork is resolved | Platform over-specialisation, which caps the career below leadership; the counterweight is finance fluency plus deliberate cross-functional scope                                    | The deepest and best-surveyed adjacent market (Salesforce Ben 2026, n=2,316 across 76 countries), also the most crowded                  | Deep technical architecture, or people management - hard to stay both |
| Enablement                      | Spots what top performers do and teaches it                            | Sellers and marketers who scaled peer training, adult-learning backgrounds | 3-7, plus the adult-learning craft                                                         | Its exits lead out of RevOps, so the RevOps ladder has to be re-entered deliberately rather than climbed continuously                                                                | Not separately sized; demand tracks sales-headcount growth rather than ops hiring                                                        | GTM management, product or customer training                          |
| Deal Desk                       | Has patience and balances rep pressure against business and legal risk | Junior finance or accounting, marketing coordinator, HR, admin             | 3-7, but only after transiting another track first                                         | The only track whose documented exits are lateral - a management seat means becoming an analyst or a systems owner on the way                                                        | Thin: a deal desk exists only where deal complexity justifies staffing one                                                               | Stay in deal ops, or move to analyst or systems work                  |

Both ties are real, and effort breaks each.

Systems manager and enablement match on calendar years, because each adds a rung of its own craft before the management fork opens at all. They diverge on effort because one of those crafts still counts at the ceiling and the other must be partly re-learned.

Analyst and project manager match because they share a ladder rung and a feeder population (the project-manager track's own feeder is ex-analysts). Study hours separate them, not years.

Default track: whichever of project manager and analyst the archetype quadrant already fits, since both reach a management seat with no new stack. Move up to the systems track once the candidate already owns platform depth, and not before.

What this order starves: the systems track pursued to architecture depth. It carries the highest ceiling of the five (its archetype's growth wing is product management and CTO-style leadership) and the longest runway, so it loses every efficiency round it enters.

Promote it whole, ratio ignored, on any of three conditions:

- The candidate already owns the platform depth, the most common door into RevOps.
- The target employers are enterprise-scale, since a genuine Staff or Principal IC track exists mainly above roughly 500 employees.
- The candidate builds with AI rather than merely uses it, because that premium (up to $60,000 over generalist peers per RevOps Co-op 2026 salary report) lands on the build side.

Delete a ruled-out track rather than parking it last, where it returns as a hedge and dilutes the roadmap.

A candidate who named stakeholder work and change management as what they avoid loses the project-manager and enablement tracks. One whose geography excludes enterprise-scale employers loses the deep-IC architecture branch, because that ladder does not exist below roughly 500 employees.

Say which track was deleted and why - a candidate who never hears it deleted keeps applying to it.

The ordering is a default, not a law: it shifts with who executes it. Re-rank against all three factors below, and say which answer moved which track:

1. The entry-path matrix moves it first - a candidate arriving with SQL is not paying the analyst track's study cost, and one arriving as an admin is not paying the systems track's platform cost.
2. The archetype quadrant moves it second, since Barol's advice is to lean into the superpower and hire for the gap.
3. Market depth moves it last: a thin market means fewer shots per year whatever the order says.

## Path-shape archetypes

Sarah Bell's board-game analogies (Salesforce Ben, published 2021-08-04, updated 2025-01-13) exist to normalize non-linear paths, because a RevOps career "doesn't have to follow a straight, upward direction."

- **Monopoly** - slow, steady, credential-driven climb.
- **Chutes and Ladders** - curiosity-driven zig-zag, willing to move laterally to chase interest.
- **Battleship** - long-horizon, stays put, moves only for the most strategic opportunity.
- **Yahtzee** - generalist, chases variety of roles over depth.
- **Apples to Apples** - relationship-first, built on network and reading the room rather than tooling depth.

Ask which shape matches the candidate's last two or three moves. It reframes an apologetic career story as a deliberate one, and it predicts what kind of next move will actually satisfy them.

## Entry-path transfer matrix

RevOps is a migrated-into discipline. It barely existed as a named function before 2015-2016, so nearly everyone senior today built their career elsewhere first. The widest single door is Sales Ops: "roughly 19.5% came from sales operations, making it the most common entry path" (Revenue Operations Alliance, cited by The GTM Advisor Group).

| From                                    | Transfers                                                               | Must close                                                          | Practitioner note                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| SDR / AE                                | Deal-cycle empathy, knowing where process breaks, credibility with reps | CRM architecture, data modeling, SQL, systems thinking              | Often skip the analyst rung and enter as managers; quota credibility is real but the technical gap blocks |
| CRM / platform admin                    | Systems configuration, field governance, automation, data hygiene       | Commercial acumen, forecasting, cross-functional influence          | Accidental entry is common - pulled in as the savviest system user                                        |
| Business / data analyst                 | SQL, dashboards, requirements gathering, analytical rigor               | GTM process mechanics, stakeholder and change management            | Fits the analyst track; may branch to data science instead                                                |
| Finance / FP&A                          | Revenue modeling, capacity and quota planning, unit economics           | CRM and systems depth, GTM process, tooling                         | Modeling skills translate directly; often excels at forecasting                                           |
| CS ops                                  | Lifecycle and retention metrics, post-sale process, customer empathy    | Top-of-funnel mechanics, pipeline and attribution, CRM architecture | Expands from post-sale into full funnel                                                                   |
| Marketing ops                           | Marketing stack, campaign execution, attribution, data infrastructure   | Sales process mechanics, CS metrics, forecasting                    | Systems thinking transfers directly; scope widens to full lifecycle                                       |
| Management consulting                   | Structured problem-solving, executive communication, project management | Hands-on technical and tooling depth                                | "Trade-off is usually less hands-on technical depth"                                                      |
| Outside GTM (IT, engineering, changers) | API integration, database and systems architecture                      | Commercial acumen, cross-functional communication, GTM domain       | Bring architecture, need commercial acumen                                                                |

The platform-admin path is the best documented.

What transfers:

- Permissions and access management.
- Report building.
- Cross-org exposure.

What is missing, per Brad Smith (CEO and co-founder, Sonar and WizOps, writing on Salesforce Ben): "Admins are often focused on knocking out requests and have little to no involvement in setting the strategy. But ops teams play an active role in establishing the strategy."

Red flags that ops is the wrong move for a given admin:

- Loving pure execution.
- Disliking project management.
- Thinking in an engineer-first way.

## Competency inventory

Map the candidate against all five groups, not just the technical ones. The Revenue Operations Certification (Revenue Operations Associates with RevOps Co-op) organizes RevOps competence into what its authors call the five food groups:

- **Process** - the closed-loop acquisition, retention and expansion system.
- **People** - cross-functional collaboration across sales, marketing, product, finance and IT.
- **Technology** - navigating the GTM tool ecosystem without becoming a data janitor.
- **Finance** - the math of growth; speaking in revenue and margin terms.
- **Data** - turning messy inputs into knowledge that powers forecasting, pricing and automation.

The financial-fluency bar, from Stephen Diorio (Partner, Revenue Operations Associates): "Show me a RevOps person who can manage data and speak the language of finance, and I'll show you somebody who is in the boardroom in three weeks."

The most-named soft-skill gap is narrower than "communication." Jared Myatt (Senior Director of Revenue Operations, Integrate), asked what the biggest non-technical gap is: "I think for me personally, it's humility mixed with self-awareness. Being able to know when you're wrong and pivot." The mechanism he names is attachment - operators build the systems and processes themselves, so reversing a decision feels like reversing themselves.

## Certifications and what they are actually worth

Naming credentials and their issuing ecosystems is appropriate here because the credential itself is the subject. Everywhere else in this skill, describe work by category, not by vendor.

The verdict column already holds the return ranking; these are its axes, with the three credentials the sources give an identical verdict grouped as _the no-uplift credentials_ (Pavilion's programmes, the ROC, Revenue Architecture).

- efficiency (measured pay step per study hour): Advanced Administrator > Platform App Builder > the free RevOps foundation > Administrator (ADM 201) > the enterprise specialisations > the no-uplift credentials
- value (what it buys at the ceiling): the enterprise specialisations > Advanced Administrator > Platform App Builder > Administrator (ADM 201) > the no-uplift credentials == the free RevOps foundation
- effort (study and preparation time): the enterprise specialisations > Advanced Administrator == Platform App Builder > Administrator (ADM 201) > the no-uplift credentials > the free RevOps foundation

Each tie is argued. On value, the no-uplift credentials and the free foundation both buy framework vocabulary and no located pay uplift; a fee and a network separate them, and neither changes the ceiling reached. On effort, Advanced Administrator and Platform App Builder are consecutive tiers of one ladder with the same 6-18%-per-tier shape, so either is one more tier of the same preparation.

Default: the free foundation for anyone entering from outside the ecosystem, then Advanced Administrator for anyone whose seat touches the CRM daily. Move to Platform App Builder only once the Advanced Administrator premium has actually landed in an offer or a review - the stacking claim is a vendor figure, not a survey.

What this order starves: the enterprise specialisation stack, the only credentials the sources still describe as climbing on demand and pay, and a year-two-to-three commitment that therefore loses every efficiency round. Promote it when the candidate targets enterprise-bound seats where those systems _are_ the surface being governed.

Delete the vendor rows outright for a candidate whose target seats do not run that platform, rather than ranking it last where it reappears as a plan item. A credential for a stack nobody at the target uses is study time with no consumer.

Two findings to state plainly rather than bury:

1. Across the wider ecosystem, moving up a certification tier correlates with a 6-18% salary increase, and 65% of admin respondents believe certifications raise their earning potential while 35% disagree (Salesforce Ben 2026 salary survey, n=2,316 across 76 countries, published 2026-03-23).
2. Certification volume thins with seniority: 16.9% of senior professionals hold none, versus 9.8% of juniors, which reads as credentials mattering less once hands-on experience is proven.

Above entry level, hands-on experience beats credentials, and hiring managers actively probe behind them. From a RevOps Co-op hiring-manager write-up: "a certification isn't always proof of competency... We will ask questions to verify that you have the practical experience." Getting defensive when questioned about a credential is itself a negative signal.
