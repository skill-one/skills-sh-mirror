# NDA-safe case study: template, example, negative example

Every number in a case study must be the candidate's own evidence, converted to NDA-safe form (percentage change, index with baseline = 100, or an aggregated range). Never invent figures to make a template look complete - an interviewer probing one fabricated number sinks the whole portfolio.

## Template

```
# [Descriptor client] - [one-line outcome, indexed or percentage]

**Quick facts:** role · engagement type (agency/in-house/freelance) · timeline ·
team size · channels · one-line impact (indexed).

## Problem
What was broken or missing, in business terms. 2-3 sentences.

## Process
What you investigated, what you found, what you decided to test and why.
This section carries the hiring signal - it shows judgment, not access to budget.

## Solution
What you actually changed: structure, targeting, creative approach, measurement.
Category concepts, not one vendor's menu paths.

## Results (indexed, baseline = 100)
| Metric | Before | After | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| [metric] | 100 | [index] | [+/-X%] |

## Learnings
One thing that worked, one that did not, one thing you would do differently.
The failure line is deliberate - it shows self-awareness, not weakness.

*All client details anonymised; figures indexed to protect confidential data.*
```

## Worked example (illustrative - every value below is a placeholder shape, not a real result; replace with your own evidence)

# DTC apparel brand - cut acquisition cost by [X]% while holding volume

**Quick facts:** Paid social buyer · agency engagement · [N] months · team of 2 · paid social + retargeting · CPA index 100 → [index], conversion volume held within [±Y]%.

**Problem.** Acquisition cost had risen for [N] consecutive months while spend held flat; the account was concentrated in a handful of aging creatives and one broad audience.

**Process.** Audited spend concentration and creative age; found [share]% of spend in creatives older than [N] weeks with frequency climbing. Ranked hypotheses: creative fatigue first, audience saturation second, landing page third - tested in that order.

**Solution.** Built a weekly creative-testing cadence ([N] net-new concepts/week), restructured toward consolidated campaigns to pool learning, moved reporting from platform dashboard to blended cost per new customer.

**Results (indexed, baseline = 100).**

| Metric                | Before | After   | Change |
| --------------------- | ------ | ------- | ------ |
| CPA (blended)         | 100    | [index] | [-X%]  |
| New-customer volume   | 100    | [index] | [±Y%]  |

**Learnings.** The testing cadence mattered more than any single winning ad; the first restructure moved too fast and briefly destabilised delivery - the second was staged over two weeks.

## Negative example - what disqualifies a case study

The same story, told the way that loses interviews and can breach an NDA:

> "At **[real client name]** I managed their Meta account spending **$85K/month**. Here's a screenshot of the ads manager showing the account name and revenue. ROAS went from 1.4 to 3.8 - screenshot attached. I can share the full account audit deck I made for them."

What is wrong, line by line:

- The real client name: NDA breach.
- The absolute spend and revenue figures: confidential data.
- The raw ads-manager screenshot with account name visible: legal risk, and interviewers read it as a preview of how their data would be treated.
- Offering a client's internal deck: breach on its face.

The anonymised version above carries the same credibility - "DTC apparel brand", indexed results, described process - with none of the exposure.
