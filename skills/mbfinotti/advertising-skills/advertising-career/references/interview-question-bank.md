# Interview question bank

Verified questions from candidate reports (Glassdoor), hiring-manager-sourced lists (Growth.Talent, compiled from 20+ growth leaders, 2026; Senator We Run Ads, 2024), and named practitioners. Grouped by format, with sample answers where the source material supports one.

## Live audit / account teardown

The interviewer screen-shares an account (real or example) and asks the candidate to audit it and recommend. Agency owner Jason Parks (The Media Captain): "ask the person you're interviewing to do a screen share with you where they audit your Google Ads account... It's a vote of confidence to see someone confidently dissecting your Google Ads account." The failure he screens for: "It's easy to send off a generic audit generated by third-party software" - a tool audit read aloud fails the round.

A defensible spoken structure, ~10 minutes:

1. Orient (1 min): business model, conversion action, what "good" means for this account before touching any setting.
2. Money first (3 min): where spend concentrates, whether the top spenders hit target CPA/ROAS, the single biggest waste line.
3. Structure and signal (3 min): campaign/ad-group logic, conversion tracking sanity, bidding fit to the goal.
4. Prioritised recommendation (2 min): the top three changes, ordered by expected impact, each with the metric that would prove it worked.
5. What you'd need next (1 min): the data you could not see and would request - this reads as rigor, not weakness.

## Mental-maths / unit-economics case

Glassdoor Paid Media Analyst report: "The case interviews ask you to use mental math to calculate metrics down the purchasing funnel such as CTR, CPC and then to compare 2 companies' revenues and gross profits... Group interview is an excel case with similar questions."

Drill the chain both directions until fluent: impressions → CTR → clicks → CPC → spend → conversion rate → conversions → CPA → AOV/ACV → revenue → gross margin → profit. Practise saying the intermediate numbers out loud; the round tests composure as much as arithmetic.

## Metric-diagnosis scenarios

Verified questions:

- "Your campaign has a high CTR but a low conversion rate. What could be the possible reasons and how would you address them?"
- "A client's campaign is not delivering impressions. What could be the reasons and how would you fix it?"
- "You notice a decline in the Quality Score of a top-performing keyword. What steps would you take?"
- "Here's our funnel data. Where's the biggest opportunity?" - the tell: look for the biggest **absolute** drop-off, not the biggest percentage drop.
- "This A/B test shows a 5% lift with p=0.08. What do you do?"
- "What would you do with a $50K/month paid media budget?"
- Scaling diagnosis: "I duplicated the campaign and set the new budget to $5,000 per day [from $2,000]. The higher budget yielded a much lower ROAS and almost doubled our CPM... why?" - expected answer: audience saturation / diminishing marginal returns; the algorithm reaches beyond the efficient core of the audience to spend the larger budget.
- B2B / professional-network variants: "The client wants to target decision-makers in a specific industry. How would you set this up?"; "You are tasked with creating a retargeting campaign. What audience segments would you target?"
- DTC / paid-social variant: "You notice that ad fatigue is affecting performance. How would you address this?"

"CPA doubled overnight" and "ROAS up, revenue flat" framings appear in agency thought-leadership rather than confirmed interview transcripts - still worth preparing with the same structure.

### Sample answer: paid-social ROAS drop

The practitioner-sourced diagnostic order, worth stating as an order rather than a list - and worth saying out loud in the round that it is ordered by hit rate per minute of checking, not by likelihood alone:

- efficiency: creative health > audience saturation > site-side conversion > attribution accuracy
- effort: site-side conversion (a cross-team ask, someone else's release log) > attribution accuracy (a tracking audit) > audience saturation > creative health (both visible in the account in minutes)

1. **Creative health** - fatigue is the cause roughly 60-70% of the time; check frequency and the age of the top spenders first.
2. **Audience saturation** - rising CPM against flat reach; the scaling-diagnosis question above is this in disguise.
3. **Site-side conversion** - did the landing page, offer, price, or checkout change? The ad account cannot fix a site problem.
4. **Attribution accuracy** - did tracking, consent, or the attribution window change? A reporting artefact is not a performance drop.

Open by scoping the window: a drop measured over a week or less is often noise - establish the comparison period before diagnosing. Close by naming the business metric that decides it (blended MER or contribution, not the platform dashboard alone).

### Sample answer: 5% lift at p=0.08

Do not answer "significant" or "not significant" - answer with a decision frame: what is the cost of being wrong in each direction, and can the test run longer to reach the pre-agreed threshold?

- If the variant is cheap to keep and the downside is small, ship it and keep measuring.
- If the decision is expensive or irreversible, extend the test.

Naming that the threshold should have been set before the test reads as senior.

## Take-home case study

The verified controversy: companies brief "a few hours" or "max 4 hours"; candidates report 12 to 30+ hours, and one priced a task at $2,800 of billable time. Idea theft is documented - candidates report seeing unpaid presentation ideas implemented after being ghosted. Ask A Manager's standard: "It's reasonable to ask candidates to spend an hour or two... it's not reasonable to ask them to complete complex projects, at least not without pay."

Rules:

- Time-box to 2-4 hours, and say so in the deliverable.
- State every assumption explicitly.
- Lead with the reasoning, not production polish.
- Push back on any brief that amounts to a full go-to-market plan for an unlaunched product - offer a scoped alternative ("I've focused the 3 hours on the channel and measurement plan; a full plan would need X, Y, Z from your data").

## Behavioural / client round

A reported three-stage shape: HR screen, then a campaign-manager technical round ("What KPIs would you look for when assessing the results and how would you then report it?"), then an executive character round.

Prepare each story in three lengths - full (2 minutes), short (60 seconds), one line (15 seconds) - so the same evidence survives a follow-up or a rapid-fire round. Bank stories across ownership, conflict/client management, failure and what changed, and a result tied to a business number.

Structure each as situation (1-2 sentences), task (1), action (2-3), result with metric (1-2). Use the candidate's own evidence only - never invent figures to fill a story.
