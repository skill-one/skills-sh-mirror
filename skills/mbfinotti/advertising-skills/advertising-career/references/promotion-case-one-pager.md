# Promotion-case one-pager

The ladder pays for accountability, not time served: cases citing owned revenue or P&L impact succeed; cases citing tenure, hours, campaign counts, or effort fail. Scope creep is the modal experience - Marketing Week's 2026 Career & Salary Survey (2,350 respondents) found 51.2% of marketers saw their remit grow without matching pay - which makes it a legitimate lever, but only once converted into an owned-number argument.

Every figure in the one-pager is the candidate's own evidence; market anchors carry source, date, and grade (survey vs aggregate). An undated or last-year anchor in a declining market damages credibility.

## Template

```
# Case for [target title], [name], [date]

## What I own now that I didn't at [start of period]
- [Scope item 1: e.g. budget/forecast responsibility for X accounts / £Y band]
- [Scope item 2: e.g. measurement ownership - tracking, attribution, first
  incrementality test]
- [Scope item 3: e.g. QA or mentoring of N juniors]

## What the owned number did
[1-3 lines: the revenue, pipeline, contribution, or efficiency number you are
accountable for, and its movement over the period - your own evidence,
indexed if the absolute figure is confidential internally.]

## Evidence against the next rung's screen
[Map each item above to what the next level is screened for: forecasts and
budgets, C-level narrative, cross-channel allocation, incrementality - cite
the internal ladder or a real JD for the target title.]

## Market anchor
[Target-title range, with currency, source, date, and grade. State the grade
out loud: "survey-grade" or "aggregate estimate, treat as a range".]

## The ask
[Title, band, and timing. One sentence.]
```

## Positive example (shape)

"Since January I have owned the paid-media budget and quarterly forecast for our three largest accounts - responsibility that sat with my director a year ago - and ran our first geo-holdout test, which changed how we allocate between search and social. The number I'm accountable for is [owned metric and its movement]. That maps to the senior screen - forecast ownership, measurement depth, portfolio economics. The 3Search 2025 survey (1,300+ responses) puts UK PPC Managers at £50,000-£70,000; I'm asking for [band] effective [date]."

Why it works:

- Every claim is an owned scope item or an owned number.
- The evidence is mapped to the next rung's documented screen.
- The anchor is dated and graded.

## Negative example

"I've been here four years, I've never missed a deadline, I ran over 200 campaigns this year and regularly stay late during launches. Everyone else at my level has been promoted. I'd like the senior title."

Why it fails:

- Four years is tenure, not evidence.
- 200 campaigns and late nights are activity, not evidence.
- Peer comparison is not evidence.
- Nothing maps to what senior is screened for: no owned budget, no forecast, no number.

This case reads as a request for recognition, not a demonstration of accountability, and it fails even when the underlying work was genuinely strong.
