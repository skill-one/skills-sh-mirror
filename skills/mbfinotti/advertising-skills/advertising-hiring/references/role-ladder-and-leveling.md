# Role Ladder and Leveling

## The functional ladder

Titles are inconsistent; the ladder underneath them is not. Name the rung in writing before drafting anything else.

| Rung                         | Core scope                                                            | Owns                              |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Media buyer / PPC specialist | Channel execution: setup, bids, placements, pacing, optimization      | Channel KPIs (ROAS, CPA, CPC)     |
| Paid media manager           | Cross-channel budget allocation, reporting, sometimes team lead       | Blended channel performance       |
| Performance marketer         | Adds funnel strategy, creative testing, measurement, business outcome | CAC, MER, contribution margin     |
| Creative strategist          | Turns research and performance data into ad concepts and briefs       | Hook rate, CTR, creative win rate |
| Growth marketer              | Whole system: acquisition, conversion, lifecycle                      | System-level growth, LTV:CAC      |

Darkroom draws the sharpest boundary: "Performance marketing measures channels. Growth marketing measures the system." Performance marketing "ends at the channel boundary. It optimizes what happens inside [each ad platform]. It does not optimize what happens between those channels. It does not optimize the conversion experience. It does not optimize the post-purchase lifecycle." Its operational test: a media buyer plus an account manager is performance marketing; a strategist, media team, CRO resource, lifecycle marketer, and analyst working in coordination is growth marketing.

The table defines the rungs; it does not order them for a hiring decision. For a first dedicated hire, order by business outcome owned per week of ramp - `performance marketer > media buyer > creative strategist > growth marketer` - and make the generalist operator the default. Value and effort both disagree with that order, which is exactly the point - the default is neither the broadest seat nor the fastest one to fill:

- value (breadth the seat can own): `growth marketer > performance marketer > creative strategist > media buyer`
- effort (time-to-fill, ramp length, panel time, comp pressure): `growth marketer > creative strategist > performance marketer > media buyer`

The default moves on a named condition, never on preference:

- A working funnel needing depth in one channel moves it down to a media buyer.
- ~$50k/month of spend, or 3+ channels, adds a buyer alongside the generalist.
- Creative volume as the bottleneck opens a separate creative-strategist req.
- Conversion and lifecycle already in scope moves it up to a growth marketer.

Re-rank against the company's own assets too - an existing analyst or CRO resource makes the growth-marketer rung cheaper to fill and to ramp than this default assumes.

Remoby sets the honest ceiling on the bottom rung: "buyers optimize what exists; they do not rescue a broken product, rebuild messaging, or invent positioning from scratch." In small teams one person is all three rungs; titles blur most in mid-sized companies, and both Daphne Tideman and Talentfoot warn that companies "want to hire a growth marketer when that might not be what the company actually needs."

## Creative strategist: separate role, separate req

Constant Hire: the role sits "at the intersection of research, creative direction, and performance," preventing the failure of "designers producing assets, media buyers spending budgets, and no one owning the strategy tying it all together." The adjacent confusion, same source: "Creative Strategists drive performance learning; Creative Directors drive brand leadership. Most hiring mistakes come from misdiagnosing the bottleneck."

Foxwell Digital on skimping: "Your creative strategist is the person steering the ship. If you go cheap, you're basically handing the wheel to someone who just got their learner's permit" - "the fastest way to light your ad budget on fire." US creative strategy directors run $180,000-$250,000+ plus bonuses (Foxwell Digital, 2026, directional).

Foxwell also argues - recorded as a paraphrase, not a verbatim quote - that agency experience beats brand-side experience for strategist hires, on broader exposure and adaptability.

## Breakpoints and the hire-at-all gate

- **~$50k/month spend** - "a common breakpoint where a dedicated buyer starts paying for themselves" (Remoby).
- **3+ paid channels** - "once a team runs three or more paid channels... a generalist starts dropping balls" (Remoby).
- **$1M-$5M annual revenue, DTC** - "a single paid media specialist or small agency handling one or two channels is often sufficient" (Darkroom).
- **Don't hire outside help at all** (AJ Wilcox; scoped to one B2B social channel but the logic generalizes):
  - Monthly channel budget under $5,000.
  - Product LTV under $10,000.
  - Marketing fundamentals missing (audience, positioning, pricing, working site).
  - Wanting a hands-off vendor rather than a partner.
  - Unwillingness to commit 3-6 months before judging channel fit - this last condition doubles as a hiring warning: an organization unwilling to wait that long will fire the hire before the ramp completes.

## When the gate fails: the alternatives, ranked

Ranked by channel coverage bought per hour of the founder's or marketing leader's own time - `agency > freelancer > self-manage`, with `wait` reserved for the case where the missing fundamental is the actual blocker. Lead with the agency because it returns the most coverage for the fewest of your hours, not because it is the cheapest line item; it is not.

| Option      | What it buys                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | What it costs you                                                                                               |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Agency      | Several channels, creative bandwidth, and cover when one person is out. SotaMedia, without talent snobbery: "The two models do not differ in talent... Agency wins on data velocity, because it runs many accounts in parallel" | A briefing cadence measured in hours a month, contract and account-access review, and the most control given up |
| Freelancer  | One or two channels run competently, reversible in a notice period                                                                                                                                                              | Day-to-day direction from you, a single point of failure, narrow scope                                          |
| Self-manage | Full control and every learning stays in the building                                                                                                                                                                           | A standing job for someone who already has one; the expertise has to exist or be built                          |
| Wait        | Nothing, on purpose - the right call while positioning, pricing, or a working site is the real constraint                                                                                                                       | The channel stays unrun for as long as the fundamental is missing                                               |

- value (channels covered, creative bandwidth, absence cover): `agency > freelancer > self-manage`
- effort (your hours briefing, reviewing, coordinating): `self-manage > freelancer > agency`
- compliance cost (contract review, third-party access to ad accounts and customer data, contractor classification): `freelancer > agency > self-manage` - not a tie: both third parties need the same contract and the same account access, but only the individual engagement raises a worker-classification question, and only that one is awkward to unwind once the work has been done.

The order is a default, not a law. `Wait` is the case where the whole menu is deleted rather than reordered - say the hire is off the table until the missing fundamental is fixed, because a bottom-ranked hire is one budget meeting away from happening anyway.

Re-rank the rest against what the company already has:

- An in-house marketer with real channel hours moves self-manage up.
- An agency already retained on another workstream collapses the agency's setup effort to near-zero.
- A founder who has run the accounts personally makes self-manage the cheapest bridge rather than the most expensive.

In-house wins on product knowledge and is the destination once the gate passes - these are the bridge to it, not a replacement.

The most-cited founder error when weighing them (SotaMedia) is "comparing the wrong numbers: agency retainer fees against in-house salaries. That comparison tells you almost nothing about ROI." Compare hours returned and channels covered instead.

## First hire versus specialist, and other context splits

Demand Curve: "The mistake companies make is hiring a marketer without first having an intuition around how their company is going to grow. Marketers vary drastically in their... skill sets."

| Context                       | What the hire must do differently                                       | Common mis-hire                                                         |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| First ad hire (no team)       | Stand up tracking and attribution, choose channels, own the full funnel | A narrow single-channel specialist who cannot build from scratch        |
| Adding a specialist to a team | Go deep in one channel, plug into existing measurement                  | Another generalist when depth was needed                                |
| Agency to in-house            | Own one brand deeply, work without a pod, absorb product context        | Agency multitasker who cannot go deep or misses pod support             |
| B2C/DTC to B2B                | Optimize to pipeline and SQLs over long cycles                          | DTC buyer optimizing to first-order profitability on a long sales cycle |
| B2B to DTC                    | Creative-testing velocity, contribution-margin math, fast data reads    | B2B/brand buyer slow on creative iteration and margin discipline        |

## Spend under management: filter, not level

Job postings do anchor level to spend - one senior media buyer posting reads "You will be managing upwards of $50,000 - $75,000 a day" (Client Accelerators), and "Managing six- or seven-figure ad budgets? That's another pay booster" (MarketerHire). Practitioners treat the number as directional only, because running a large automated budget is not the same as producing incremental profit.

The counterweight screen (Constant Hire): "Ask them to walk through a campaign they killed and why... how they separate incremental revenue from attribution modeling noise. Answers that stay at the level of platform features tell you they have executed someone else's media strategy rather than set their own." Unpaired, the spend number is meaningless.

## Salary bands - directional, quote with source and date

Best practitioner source: the Duane Brown / Take Some Risk anonymous PPC salary survey, 2026 edition, 445 responses across 50+ countries, reported in Search Engine Land ("PPC salaries are splitting," Benjamin Wenner, April 13, 2026).

US medians by experience:

- 3-5 years: $87,500.
- 6-9 years: $100,000.
- 10-15 years: $135,000.
- 15+ years: $150,000.

In-house out-earns agency at every band (10-15 years: $140,000 versus $123,545); the 6-9 year in-house figure ($170,000) is flagged by the same report as a small, outlier-skewed sample, so never quote it unqualified. Freelance median at 10-15 years is $202,895, "a $79,000 premium for going independent."

Non-US, same survey: UK 10-15 years £50,000, down from £60,000; EU 10-15 years €65,625. 3Search's 2025 Performance Marketing Salary Guide (1,300+ responses) puts UK PPC managers at £50,000-£70,000, up about 7.7% over 12 months.

A full-time in-house paid-social hire runs "$70,000-$90,000 annually, depending on experience" (MarketerHire, circa 2026). Aggregators disagree by tens of thousands depending on role label, and the survey is self-selected with a shrinking sample (445 in 2026, down from 715 in 2022). Every figure is directional; always attach source and date.

The leveling insight behind the bands (Search Engine Land, same report): the earners pulling away "have repositioned from campaign operators to business outcome owners... Speak in revenue contribution and margin impact, not ROAS and CTR" - the middle of the market is "being squeezed from below by automation absorbing execution work." Weight the scorecard toward outcome ownership accordingly.

## The unresolved generalist-versus-specialist split

This one is deliberately left unranked, and that is the answer, not an omission. The sources agree an undifferentiated generalist is bad, but the fix genuinely differs:

- One hiring guide argues for category-specific full-stack operators who own both strategy and execution.
- Another argues a single person cannot run paid social alone, and recommends splitting into manager, creative, and analyst roles as budget allows.

Ordering the two would be false precision: they are not two routes to one outcome, they are two different bets on how the team grows, and the published evidence does not favour either. Present both to the user and let the first-hire-versus-specialist test, plus the breakpoints above, settle it.
