# Work Sample Design

The richest first-hand source is the PPCChat "Hiring & Getting Hired in PPC" roundup (April 19, 2022), which devoted a full question to test projects. Its verdict is unusually one-sided.

## Unpaid take-homes actively damage the funnel

Anu Adegbola's summary of the community position: "test projects during the interviews are mostly hated by the community."

- Sarah Stemen: "At this point in my career I have a NO HOMEWORK policy."
- Julia Vyse (senior media buyer): "I hate this. It's straight-up time-wasting, unpaid labor... it should not have a role in hiring."
- Melissa Mackey: "doing free work for someone who may or may not hire me? No thanks. And frankly, they're not great from the employer side either, too hard to proctor."
- Robert Brady (Righteous Marketing): "The 'findings' from a project should be discoverable in an interview (or two). If not, you need to improve the interview process."
- Julie Bacchini (Neptune Moon): "If you are asking someone to do work on an account... you should compensate them for their time."

This matters commercially, not just ethically: senior practitioners decline unpaid exercises, so issuing one silently shrinks the funnel to candidates with uncommitted hours.

## The formats, ranked

Pick by signal returned per candidate-hour, net of who declines rather than comply: `live screen-share audit > data-table case > own-work presentation > paid trial > job shadow`. The multi-hour unpaid take-home is not on this list at any position - it is the one format whose funnel cost exceeds its signal.

| Format                                                                      | What it returns                                                               | What it costs                                                          |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Live screen-share account audit                                             | Method exposed in real time, immune to audit tooling and generated output     | An hour of the interview plus a panelist able to grade an audit live   |
| Data-table case (Marc Barraza's variant)                                    | Reasoning about metric relationships, gradeable by anyone with the answer key | An hour, and a table built in advance                                  |
| Own-work presentation (Tim Jensen)                                          | Depth on work they actually did, no scheduling friction, near-zero drop-off   | Their framing, their choice of case - you see the wins, not the misses |
| Paid trial or paid consulting engagement (Andrew McGarry)                   | The strongest signal available: real work, real constraints                   | A week of calendar, a budget approval, pay terms, and account access   |
| Job shadow day, "no teacher/student dynamic" (Mike Ryan, Smarter Ecommerce) | Working style and how they ask questions                                      | A day of a team member's time for a diffuse read                       |

- value (how much of their actual method you see): `paid trial > live screen-share audit > data-table case > own-work presentation > job shadow`
- effort (scheduling, panel hours, prep, budget approval, calendar drag): `paid trial > job shadow > live screen-share audit == data-table case > own-work presentation`
- candidate drop-off (seniors who decline rather than comply): `unpaid take-home > paid trial > job shadow > live screen-share audit == data-table case > own-work presentation`
- compliance cost (pay terms and worker classification for real work; access to live accounts and customer data): `paid trial > job shadow > live screen-share audit > own-work presentation > data-table case`

The two ties are structural, not undecided: the audit and the data-table case both consume one hour of an already-scheduled interview plus one prepared thing, and neither asks for an hour outside the loop, which is the only thing that drives drop-off. They separate on compliance, so that axis breaks them - an own-work presentation invites a former employer's account data into your room, which a synthetic table never does.

What this order starves is the paid trial: first on value and first on effort, so a ratio ranks it last forever while the in-interview audit wins on being cheap to run. Promote it on purpose for a senior hands-on seat, for a creative strategist whose portfolio cannot be verified as their own, or when a candidate already contracting elsewhere makes a week of their time cheap to buy.

This ordering is a default, not a law, and it moves with who is running the loop.

Delete rather than demote what your constraints rule out, and name the deletion:

- No panelist who can grade an audit live removes the top row.
- Accounts that cannot be exposed to an outsider remove it too.
- No budget or approval for paid work removes the paid trial and the job shadow outright.

Promotions work the same way in reverse - an existing anonymized dataset from a previous search collapses the data-table case's prep to near-zero.

Whichever row wins, keep it time-boxed and inside the interview:

- Duane Brown: "Testing within the 60-minute interview is fine. We do it. I wouldn't ask someone to take stuff home or do a 4-hour media plan for me."
- DigitalSamIAm (agency owner): "We have a test exercise, takes 90 minutes. I'm happy to pay for someone's dinner or whatever during it... Gives us a chance to evaluate how candidates think + handle challenges within a fixed time period."
- Ekta: "The sweet spot is to have a small test project with more of scenario-type problems instead of 'run 5 ads for me' type projects."
- Jon Kagan: take-homes "only for mid to senior level roles."

## The live screen-share account audit

Jason Parks (The Media Captain; published June 28, 2020, updated November 2023) describes the highest-signal exercise found: "I recommend asking the person you're interviewing to do a screen share with you where they audit your [ad] account... It's easy to send off a generic audit generated by third-party software. It's a vote of confidence to see someone confidently dissecting your [ad] account."

The live format is the whole point: it defeats both third-party audit tooling and AI-generated output. This is also why Foxwell Digital recommends paid real-world assignment tests for creative strategists, since AI-generated portfolios have made portfolio screening unreliable for judging creative-strategy skill.

Parks pairs the audit with a diagnostic prompt: "A month into our campaign, what results would raise a red flag?" - and expects a specific number (for example, ecommerce ROAS below 2:1), not a vague answer.

## Ambiguity detection is the signal, not a nuisance

Steve Gibson (PPCChat), from the candidate side of an ambiguous data case: "I... pointed out why the data wasn't conclusive enough to answer their 'What would you do...?' questions. I passed with flying colours. (They hadn't realized this, nor had any of the other candidates.)" Design cases with genuine ambiguity or insufficient data on purpose, and score naming it as a top answer. A candidate who confidently answers an underpowered question is showing the exact failure mode the job punishes.

A lighter-weight variant of the same idea is Marc Barraza's data-table exercise: present a realistic performance data table and have the candidate talk through optimization recommendations, scoring reasoning about metric relationships over rote definitions.

## Design rules

- Use synthetic or anonymized data. A take-home on a real client account leaks data and asks for free work; if real account work is unavoidable, pay for it.
- Write the rubric before the first submission, weighted toward commercial reasoning over technical recall.
- The debrief carries more signal than the artifact - reasoning walked aloud exposes method a polished deliverable hides. Budget debrief time explicitly.
- Match the format to the rung: live audit or data-table case for buyers and performance marketers; a paid concept-brief assignment plus a debrief on past creative wins and losses for creative strategists.
- Any numeric point values or time caps beyond the 60-90 minute consensus are this skill's own convention rather than a published standard - say so in the packet.
