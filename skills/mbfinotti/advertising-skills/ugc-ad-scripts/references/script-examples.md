# Script Examples

Two worked packages (one B2C, one B2B) and one negative example. Products, quotes, and sources here are invented illustrations of the format - in real use, every quote must come from the user's actual evidence, or the package gets labeled `UNVALIDATED`.

Note the scripting depth throughout: **hook and CTA are locked word-for-word** (they carry the test), while **body beats are briefed with example phrasing** the creator can say in their own words.

## Worked example 1 - B2C creator script

**Concept**

- Segment: parents of school-age kids who cook on weeknights.
- Motivation (verbatim, illustrative): "I spend more time deciding what to cook than cooking" - app-store review, 2026-03.
- Format: creator talking to camera in their kitchen, phone propped on counter.
- Offer: 14-day free trial of a meal-planning app.
- Duration: 20 seconds, ~60 words. Validation status: grounded (sources listed above).

**Hook variants** (body below stays fixed; test these against it)

| #   | Archetype               | Spoken line                                              | On-screen text                  | Opening visual                              |
| --- | ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Problem callout         | "It's 5:30 and I still don't know what we're eating."    | dinner decision fatigue is real | Creator staring into an open fridge         |
| 2   | First-person confession | "I used to plan meals in the school pickup line. Badly." | my old system: panic            | Phone screen full of screenshotted recipes  |
| 3   | Proof-first             | "Four dinners, one grocery run, zero 5 pm panic."        | this week's plan took 4 minutes | App's weekly plan filled in, held to camera |
| 4   | Question                | "What's actually for dinner tonight?"                    | the question I stopped dreading | Creator mid-chop, looks up at camera        |

**Body** (fixed across hooks; first beat adjusted per hook to extend its premise. Spoken column is example phrasing, not a locked read - the creator says it their way.)

| Time    | Spoken (example phrasing)                                                                                       | On-screen text                 | Visual                                                 |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| 3-7 s   | "The deciding was the exhausting part - not the cooking."                                                       | deciding > cooking             | Creator talks, hand on hip                             |
| 7-12 s  | "This app builds the week's dinners around what my kids will actually eat, and writes the grocery list itself." | picky-eater filter → list done | Screen recording: tap picky-eater filter, list appears |
| 12-16 s | "Sunday I swap what I don't like - five minutes, done for the week."                                            | 5 minutes on Sunday            | Creator swipes two meals away, shrugs, smiles          |

**CTA** (16-20 s)

- Spoken: "There's a two-week free trial - do one Sunday with it and see."
- On-screen: `14 days free · link below`. Visual: creator points down, plan visible behind.

**Delivery notes**

- Read direction: talk like venting to a friend, mildly amused, not salesy. No presenter energy.
- Setting: real kitchen, daylight, some clutter is good. Hold the phone plan up at beat 3.
- Pacing: cut every 2-4 seconds; no intro, first frame is the hook visual.
- Compliance: creator received free access - spoken "they gave me the app to try" folded into beat 1, plus platform paid-partnership label.

## Worked example 2 - B2B founder-led script

**Concept**

- Segment: heads of operations at 20-80 person services firms, problem-aware.
- Motivation (verbatim, illustrative): "Approvals live in email and nobody knows who's blocking" - sales-call note, 2026-05.
- Format: founder to camera, professional-network feed, 30 seconds. Teaching-first framing.
- Offer: free teardown guide "5 approval bottlenecks and fixes" (low-commitment learning ask - no demo push at this stage).
- Duration: 30 seconds. Validation status: grounded.

**Hook variants**

| #   | Archetype                    | Spoken line                                                          | On-screen text                         | Opening visual                                 |
| --- | ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Problem callout + credential | "I've watched 60 ops teams lose deals to their own approval chains." | approvals kill more deals than pricing | Founder at desk, whiteboard behind             |
| 2   | Contrarian                   | "Your approval process isn't slow because people are slow."          | it's the routing, not the people       | Founder shakes head, screen shows email thread |
| 3   | Proof-first                  | "This client cut approval time from 9 days to 1. Same team."         | 9 days → 1 day                         | Screenshot of before/after cycle-time chart    |

**Body**

| Time    | Spoken                                                                                               | On-screen text          | Visual                                   |
| ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| 3-10 s  | "The pattern is always the same: the request sits in an inbox, and nobody owns the wait."            | nobody owns the wait    | Founder talks; cut to blurred email pile |
| 10-20 s | "The fix is boring: one visible queue, one named approver per step, a clock on each one. That's it." | queue · owner · clock   | Simple 3-item list builds on screen      |
| 20-25 s | "We wrote up the five bottlenecks we see most, and the fix for each."                                | the 5 bottlenecks guide | Founder holds up printed one-pager       |

**CTA** (25-30 s)

- Spoken: "It's free, link's in the comments - steal it."
- On-screen: `free teardown · link in comments`.

**Delivery notes**

- Read direction: calm expert, first take energy, small imperfections stay in. Captions on throughout - assume sound off.
- Founder is identified as founder on-screen from second 1 ("Founder, [company]") - the connection is self-evident but label it anyway.
- Compliance: result claim in hook 3 must match a documented customer outcome; add "results vary by team" on-screen if it is not typical.
- Sequence note: this is an awareness-stage script; a buying committee will not convert off one touch. Plan the next-stage script separately.

## Negative example - what not to ship

> **Hook (spoken and on-screen, identical):** "Discover the revolutionary meal-planning solution busy families love!"
> **Body:** "MealApp is the #1 app with cutting-edge AI. Thousands of happy customers agree it will change your life. Sarah from Ohio says it cured her stress completely."
> **CTA:** "Download now! Follow us! Use code SAVE20 today only! Link in bio!"

What is wrong with it:

- Brand voice in a person's mouth: "revolutionary", "cutting-edge", "#1" - no human talks like this; it reads as an ad in the first second.
- Duplicated channels: spoken line and on-screen text are identical, and no opening visual is specified - one third of a hook.
- Hook never paid off: "discover" promises nothing specific, so the body has no premise to extend.
- Invented testimonial: no "Sarah from Ohio" exists in the evidence; a fabricated endorser is a compliance violation, not just lazy writing.
- Unsubstantiated cure-level claim: "cured her stress completely" is a health-adjacent efficacy claim with zero substantiation.
- Stacked CTAs: download + follow + code + link - four asks, so none lands.
- Fake urgency: "today only" that isn't true is a deceptive claim.
- No disclosure: paid creator reading this says nothing about the relationship.
- No delivery notes: nothing tells the creator tone, setting, props, or beats - they are left to invent.
