# Compliance Checklist

Rules that change the words on the page. This is not legal advice; regulated categories (health, finance, supplements, legal services) need their own legal review before anything ships. Regulation here moves fast - verify current text before relying on a specific figure or wording.

Ask which markets the ad runs in before writing disclosures. The rules below stack: an ad running in the US and the EU must satisfy both.

## Disclose material connections

- Disclose whenever the performer received anything - each is a material connection:
  - Payment.
  - Free or discounted product.
  - Affiliate commission.
  - Employment.
  - A family relationship.
- Put the disclosure in the ad itself, clear and hard to miss - spoken or on-screen, understandable to an ordinary viewer. A caption or bio mention is not sufficient placement.
- A platform's built-in "paid partnership" toggle may not be adequate on its own. Use the label, and write a disclosure into the script as well.
- Script lines that work: spoken "they sent it to me to try" / on-screen "Paid partnership with [brand]". Keep it plain and early.
- Vague credit ("thanks to [brand]") does not communicate the relationship - name it.
- Employee performers: disclose employment in the script ("I work at [brand] - here's how we actually use it").
- Reposting a creator's organic content as a paid ad does not remove the disclosure requirement.

## Jurisdictions

- **United States** - FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR Part 255 (revised 2023): material connections disclosed clearly and conspicuously.
- **United States** - FTC fake-reviews rule, 16 CFR Part 465 (effective October 2024): bans fabricated, AI-generated, and undisclosed insider reviews, undisclosed incentivized reviews, and fake engagement indicators. Civil penalties run tens of thousands of dollars per violation. This is why invented voice-of-customer is a legal problem, not a style problem.
- **European Union** - Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (2005/29/EC): hidden advertising is a misleading omission, and undisclosed advertorial sits on the Annex I blacklist, meaning it is unfair in all circumstances with no case-by-case defence. Both the creator and the brand can be liable, and the brand does not need editorial control to be exposed.
- **European Union** - Digital Services Act (2022/2065): platforms must offer a declaration tool and ensure commercial content is clearly labelled.
- **France** - loi n° 2023-451 (2023, amended 2024): label commercial content "publicité" or "collaboration commerciale", **in French**, visible throughout the promotion. Enforcement is active and audit failure rates have been high.
- Write the disclosure in the language of the market. A French-market ad needs the French label, not a translated afterthought.

## Actors and personas

- A paid actor presented as a customer must be disclosed as not an actual consumer - in the ad.
- Never script a fabricated testimonial or an invented "real user" story. Dramatize only composite-true stories, framed as such.

## Results and typicality

- A testimonial highlighting a benefit reads as a typical result. If the endorser's result is not typical, script the generally expected result - a bare "results not typical" disclaimer is explicitly insufficient.
- Before/after contrasts and number claims must be honest and substantiated; rewrite the script's numbers to what the advertiser can actually support.

## Claims and regulated categories

- A testimonial is not scientific evidence. Health or efficacy claims need substantiation the advertiser independently holds - otherwise soften the line or cut the claim.
- Do not assert or imply the viewer's health, financial status, or other protected attributes. Second-person framing is the usual trigger:
  - "Do you have [condition]?" gets rejected.
  - "[Condition] treatment services are available" passes.
  - Rewrite "you" phrasing out of health and finance scripts.
- Specific numeric health testimonials (a named lab value moving from one number to another) are routinely rejected outright - cut them rather than softening.
- Before/after and body-focused imagery is restricted in health and weight-loss categories.
- Health claims aimed at EU markets must match an authorized-claims list; check before scripting one.
- Fake urgency ("today only" that is not true) is a deceptive claim - write real deadlines or none.

## Script-pass checklist

Run on every finished script. Deliberately not ranked by effort: every item is a pass/fail gate, so an efficiency ordering would only read as permission to skip the expensive ones.

1. Which markets does this run in? → disclosure written for each, in each market's language.
2. Material connection? → disclosure line written into an early beat, spoken or on-screen, plus the platform label.
3. Performer not a real user? → non-consumer disclosure written into the ad.
4. Every quote traceable to real evidence? → if not, cut it; fabricated reviews carry per-violation penalties.
5. Result stated? → typical, or a generally-expected-result line added.
6. Health, efficacy, or financial claim? → substantiation confirmed with the user, second-person phrasing removed, else rewritten out.
7. Regulated category? → flag the whole package for the user's own legal review before filming.
