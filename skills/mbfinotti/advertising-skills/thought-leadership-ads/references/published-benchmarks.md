# Published benchmarks with provenance

Read this before quoting any number to the user. Every figure here is vendor-published, agency-reported, or a single-practitioner anecdote - none is independently verified. The one apparent third-party corroboration of the headline CTR figure is a company citing the original vendor, not measuring independently.

Two "vendor-published" clusters recur below and are not the same source. ZenABM's 2026 LinkedIn ABM benchmarks report backs the median CTR, median CPC, cost-per-landing-page-click, and headline correlation figures - the same report cited four different ways, not four converging sources. Fractional Demand's 2026 15-month B2B SaaS portfolio analysis (17.4M impressions, 6,280 ads, $3.5M spend) backs the CTR-lift and budget-allocation-tier figures - a genuinely separate source, directionally consistent with ZenABM's numbers but not a replication of them: the two datasets disagree on the size of the lift (6.9x vs. ZenABM's implied ~6.4x) and were built from different ad samples.

## How to judge a number before using it

1. Rank provenance: the client's own account data > independent research > platform-published > vendor or agency case study. Use the highest rank available. Never present a lower rank as settled fact.
2. Check cohort fit: platform, objective, industry, geography, audience size, and attribution window must roughly match the client's situation, or the number does not transfer.
3. Check the denominator (see trap below) before comparing any two CTRs.
4. Prefer teaching the user how to build their own baseline (2-4 weeks of their own data) over asserting any figure below.

## The denominator trap

Under an engagement objective, reported CTR counts social actions - reactions, comments, shares, follows - not just clicks to a destination. A person-fronted ad optimised for engagement will always beat a brand conversion ad on reported CTR partly as an artifact. Compare cost per landing-page click instead, on both sides.

## The number that outranks the rest

The largest disclosed dataset (ZenABM, 119 sponsored person-fronted posts, ~$300k spend, 161,256 ads across 211 companies, 2026) found reported CTR **negatively** correlated with pipeline: rho = -0.170. Whatever else in this file is quoted, quote this next to it.

## Consumer-side claims (same provenance problem)

- Platform-published short-video figures: 134% higher completion rate, 157% higher 6-second view-through, 69% higher conversion rate, 37% lower CPA (internal A/B tests from 2020-2022 auction conditions).
- A widely repeated third-party CPA comparison ($14.62 vs $23.18 across 780 campaigns) traces only to a secondary aggregator, not a primary document.
- A 2022 meta-analysis of 15 split tests reporting lower CPA for partnership-labelled ads is the platform's own research.
- Creative refresh cadences of 3-7 days (short-video) and 14-21 days (social feed) are agency-reported.
- The claim that founder-shot phone content beats polished creator content on engagement and CAC is a vendor claim, directionally consistent with the B2B authenticity finding, not independently verified.
